# Glossary

The words the dashboard uses, in plain language. Grouped by where you meet
them.

[← back to the README](../README.md) · [Getting started](../GETTING_STARTED.md)

---

## The verdicts

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Add** / **Buy** | Worth buying, or buying more of. Usually: you own too little of a good holding, or it ranks strongly |
| **Hold** | Nothing to do. Said out loud so silence never has to mean "fine" |
| **Watch** | Worth a look, not an action yet |
| **Trim** | Sell **part**. The position has grown too big for one holding, or its sector is too big. Nothing is wrong with the company |
| **Exit** | Sell **all**. The reason to own it has gone (quality collapsed, a balance-sheet red flag). **Exit !** with a ring is urgent |
| **Review** | A prompt to think, not to trade, e.g. a satellite holding has lost its trend |

## Your holdings

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Core** | A holding you mean to keep for a decade or more. Never sold because its price trend weakens; only a broken business triggers an exit |
| **Satellite** | Held for one to ten years. Same red-flag checks as core; losing its trend prompts a review, never a sale. The default |
| **Trade** | Something you bought purely to trade. The only kind sold when its momentum rank falls |
| **Average price (avg cost)** | What you paid per share on average. Makes profit, loss and stop levels real |
| **LT / ST quantity** | Shares held more than / at most 12 months. Long-term gains are taxed at 12.5% after a ₹1.25 lakh yearly exemption; short-term at 20% |
| **Stop / stop-loss** | A price below which the plan says the idea is wrong and you'd sell |
| **T1, T2** | First and second price targets in a trade plan |
| **Book** | Everything you own, added up |
| **Single-stock cap** | The most any one share should be of your book (5% by default) |
| **Sector cap** | The most any one sector should be (25% by default). Eight banks are one bet on Indian credit, not eight bets |

## Strategies and ideas

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Momentum** | Shares that have risen most over the past 9 months (skipping the latest month) tend to keep rising for a while |
| **Rank** | Position in the momentum list: 1 is strongest |
| **200 DMA** | The average closing price over the last 200 trading days. Above it, a share is in an uptrend |
| **Market regime** | Risk-on when the Nifty 500 is above its 200 DMA, risk-off when below |
| **Swing trade** | A trade held for days to a few weeks, on a pullback or a breakout |
| **Pullback / breakout** | A dip within an uptrend / a move above a recent high |
| **GARP / QARP** | Growth (or quality) at a reasonable price: companies growing well without an extreme valuation |
| **Results drift** | The idea that shares which jump on their results keep drifting the same way for weeks |
| **52-week high, low volatility** | Two other well-known factor ideas, available as backtests (see [validation](validation.md)) |

## Testing and evidence

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Backtest** | Running a rule on past data to see what it would have done |
| **Survivorship bias** | Testing only on companies that still exist today, which flatters every result. The backtests here include companies that later collapsed or delisted |
| **CAGR** | Compound annual growth rate: the steady yearly return that gets you from start to end |
| **Drawdown** | The fall from a peak to the next low. "−36% max drawdown" means at worst it was 36% below its best |
| **Sharpe ratio** | Return per unit of ups and downs. Higher is better; the index scores around 0.7 |
| **Benchmark / index** | What you'd get by simply holding the Nifty 500. Every idea is measured against it |
| **Random picks / control** | Shares chosen at random from the same list. An idea must beat these, not just the index |
| **Train / holdout** | Tune on one period, then check once on a later period you didn't look at |
| **Costs** | Brokerage, STT, stamp duty, exchange fees, GST and slippage. About 0.35% per round trip, and always included |

## Company figures

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Quality score** | 0–100 from growth, profitability, balance sheet, cash conversion and governance. Describes the business; it doesn't predict the price |
| **ROE / ROCE** | Return on equity / on capital employed: profit for each rupee the business uses |
| **Debt / equity** | Borrowing relative to shareholders' money |
| **Interest cover** | How many times profit covers interest payments. Under 2 is a warning |
| **Cash flow / profit** | Whether reported profit arrives as cash. Well under 1 for years is a warning |
| **Promoter pledge** | Shares the founders have pledged for loans. Rising pledge is a red flag: lenders can force-sell |
| **P/E, P/B** | Price divided by earnings / by book value per share |
| **EV / EBITDA** | Company value (including debt) over operating profit. Used for cyclical businesses, where P/E misleads |
| **NPA (gross / net)** | A bank's bad loans, before / after provisions |
| **CASA** | A bank's current and savings deposits: its cheapest money |
| **Capital adequacy (CAR)** | A bank's capital buffer against losses |
| **Sector checklist** | On Analyze a stock: the few figures that matter most in that company's sector |

## Futures and options (F&O)

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **Option chain** | All call and put options for a share or index, by strike price |
| **Straddle** | A call plus a put at the current price. Its cost is the move the market expects by expiry |
| **Implied volatility (IV)** | The size of moves the option prices imply, as a yearly percentage |
| **Expected range** | The close ± one expected move. On Today, the next close landed inside about 3 times in 4 |
| **PCR** | Put/call ratio of open interest. Shown as positioning only: it didn't predict direction here |
| **Open interest (OI)** | Option contracts still open. Large OI at a strike is read as support or resistance; tested here, it wasn't |
| **Max pain** | The strike where option buyers lose most. Not a useful forecast here |
| **Expiry** | The date the options settle: monthly for stocks, weekly or monthly for indices |

## Mutual funds

| Word | Means |
|---|---|
| **NAV** | A fund's price per unit |
| **Category** | The fund's SEBI category (large cap, flexi cap ...). Each fund is compared with its own |
| **AMFI code** | The unique number of a fund scheme; the exact way to name one |
| **Exit load** | A fee for selling within a set period, often a year |
